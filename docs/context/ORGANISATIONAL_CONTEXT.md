# Good Crowd Inc — Organisational Context for Website Implementation

## Purpose
This document gives Codex and website contributors organisational context derived from the supplied Good Crowd Inc business and governance materials. It is a context and publication-safety layer, not a replacement for the approved website copy, Constitution, Board-approved policies, or legal advice.

The public website must distinguish between:
1. approved public organisational facts;
2. approved website copy;
3. strategic plans and future targets;
4. internal governance/operational material;
5. unresolved or draft information.

Do not convert internal plans, targets or governance detail into public claims merely because they appear in source documents.

## Source hierarchy
When sources conflict, do not silently reconcile them. Flag the conflict for human review.

For website implementation use this practical precedence:
1. Current legal/governance facts: adopted Constitution and formally approved governance documents.
2. Public website wording: `docs/content/WEBSITE_COPY.md` and specifically approved website copy.
3. Brand expression: `docs/brand/BRAND_GUIDE.md` and supplied approved brand assets.
4. Organisational strategy/context: Business Plan and Board Introduction Pack.
5. Concept imagery: `docs/design/homepage-directionrev1.png` — primary current homepage visual direction only; `docs/design/homepage-direction.png` is retained as an older reference.

A higher-precedence source does not automatically authorise publication of confidential/internal information.

## Core organisational identity
Good Crowd Inc is a community-led not-for-profit incorporated association focused on strengthening regional communities through connection, participation and collaboration.

Its formal purpose includes events, festivals, placemaking initiatives and experiences; regional tourism and destination development; regional economic participation; support for local businesses, artists, producers, makers and community organisations; creativity and storytelling; and partnerships supporting regional growth and opportunity.

The organisation's central proposition is that purposeful, high-quality regional events and community engagement can connect neighbouring communities, attract visitors, strengthen pride of place and generate local economic benefit.

## Mission
Approved mission:

> To gather good crowds in regional Australia through purposeful events and experiences that leave lasting pride, visibility and prosperity behind.

Use this wording exactly where the formal mission is presented.

## Vision
Approved vision:

> A future where the communities of regional Australia are no longer the best kept secret — they are the destination.

Use this wording exactly where the formal vision is presented.

## Strategic pillars
For the website, standardise the expression as:

**People. Place. Prosperity.**

The underlying organisational strategy describes:

### People — Community Engagement
Bring residents and neighbouring communities together, strengthening connection, participation, social cohesion and shared identity.

### Place — Tourism & Visibility
Celebrate regional places, their landscapes, stories and culture; attract visitors; increase visibility; and help lesser-known places become destinations.

### Prosperity — Economic Development
Structure activity so economic benefit flows into local businesses, producers, makers, suppliers and communities.

Some source documents use “Places” while others use “Place”. Do not alternate between the two in the public website. The current web decision is singular **Place** unless explicitly changed by the project owner.

## Values
The four organisational values are:
- Goodness
- Connection
- Glee
- Community

Their core organisational meaning is:
- **Goodness:** create positive impact and design with legacy in mind.
- **Connection:** choose collaboration and participation over isolation or competition.
- **Glee:** make meaningful work and community activity enjoyable, uplifting and joyful.
- **Community:** put people, relationships and community benefit at the centre of decisions.

Use the approved website copy for public-facing explanatory paragraphs where available rather than treating these summaries as final marketing copy.

## Founders
Good Crowd Inc was founded by Tamara de Lange and Sarah Moore.

Approved source material describes Tamara's background across property management, retail, local government and events, and identifies her as CEO and Founder of Bloom’n Events Co in the Board Introduction Pack. It describes Sarah as a grass-roots marketing professional who left a 20-year corporate marketing career in 2025 and founded Splendid Wren Marketing.

Do not embellish biographies, add awards, qualifications, dates, achievements or titles that are not present in approved source material. Before publishing founder biographies, use the specifically approved website version if one exists.

## Not-for-profit model
The Constitution establishes Good Crowd Inc as a not-for-profit organisation operating for community benefit. Its assets and income are to be applied to its purposes, with permitted payments/reimbursements governed by the Constitution and law.

The organisation may undertake lawful commercial activities supporting its purposes, including events, ticket sales, sponsorships, fundraising, consultancy, tourism/destination initiatives, social enterprise, merchandise, grants and partnerships.

For public marketing copy, avoid oversimplified claims such as “100% of every dollar goes directly to communities” unless separately substantiated and approved.

## Governance
The Board is responsible for governance, strategic direction, financial stewardship, risk management, organisational sustainability, compliance, and protection of the organisation's reputation and values.

Governance detail is primarily internal/organisational context. The public launch website does not need to reproduce board procedures, procurement thresholds, conflict processes, registers, meeting procedures or internal templates unless a future governance/transparency section is specifically approved.

If board composition or office-bearer details are published, verify them against the current adopted Constitution and current Board records immediately before publication. Source documents contain differences in how roles/composition are described, so do not infer or reconcile those differences in code.

## Community and cultural conduct
Good Crowd Inc's Code of Conduct requires respectful, inclusive and community-led participation. It includes a commitment to cultural respect and acknowledges Traditional Custodians of the lands and waters on which Good Crowd gathers.

Any public Acknowledgement of Country wording should be specifically approved for the website rather than copied automatically from an internal Code of Conduct.

## Strategic roadmap — context only
The Business Plan describes a three-stage trajectory broadly framed as Establishment, Growth and Elevation. It includes future event concepts, expansion ambitions, partnership goals, impact reporting, potential playbook/licensing concepts and possible grants/advocacy activity.

These are strategic plans, not completed achievements.

Do not present future milestones as facts already achieved. Do not publish proposed event names, locations, attendance assumptions, funding assumptions or expansion plans unless the project owner explicitly approves them for public release.

## Impact and statistics — critical publication rule
The Business Plan contains future targets and indicative measures, including attendance, volunteer participation, community sentiment, visitor origin, media impressions, social reach, visitor spend, supplier engagement and business satisfaction.

These figures are targets/strategic assumptions, not achieved impact data.

Therefore:
- do not use them in an “Our Impact” section as historical results;
- do not animate them as if they are current achievements;
- do not add “10,000+ people reached”, “$500K+ generated”, “1M+ impressions”, or similar claims without approved evidence;
- do not transform targets into marketing claims by removing labels such as “target” or “Year 3”.

A reusable animated-number component inspired by Event Society may be implemented, but it should remain data-driven and unused for impact claims until verified, publishable data exists. It may alternatively be used for non-misleading factual counts only when those counts are approved and current.

## Financial information — internal by default
The Business Plan contains indicative revenue/expenditure ranges, revenue-model assumptions and cost categories. Treat these as internal strategic planning information unless explicitly approved for publication.

Do not surface budgets, projected revenue, sponsorship targets, grant reliance or other financial projections on the public launch website.

## Event concepts — proposed, not announced
The Business Plan contains proposed event formats and potential locations, including early community-scale and signature-event concepts. These are planning material, not necessarily public announcements.

Do not create event pages, event listings, ticket CTAs, dates, locations or “coming soon” announcements from these concepts unless specifically approved.

## Partnerships and sponsors
Good Crowd Inc intends to work with councils, government/tourism bodies, sponsors, community organisations, local businesses, suppliers, volunteers and other partners.

This supports the audience architecture of the Work With Us page. It does not authorise naming a partner, sponsor, council, funder or government body as an actual Good Crowd partner unless that relationship has been confirmed for publication.

## Impact reporting
The strategy contemplates public annual impact reporting. This can inform future information architecture and component design, but do not create fictional reports or imply reports already exist.

Build future-facing architecture so an Impact/Reports section can be introduced later without restructuring the whole site.

## Privacy, forms and data
Internal governance material recognises privacy obligations and the collection of contact, volunteer, sponsor and ticketing information. For the website:
- collect only data needed for the relevant workflow;
- validate server-side;
- protect secrets and recipient addresses through configuration;
- implement appropriate spam controls;
- do not publish internal contact details without approval;
- ensure privacy wording and any privacy policy are approved before production launch.

## Safety and accessibility
Good Crowd's governance material emphasises safe, inclusive participation and accessibility considerations in event planning. The website should reflect the same standard through WCAG 2.2 AA, keyboard accessibility, reduced-motion support, readable content, accessible forms and inclusive interaction design.

## Tone implications
The organisational material consistently supports a voice that is:
- community-led rather than bureaucratic;
- confident rather than corporate;
- optimistic without unsupported hype;
- collaborative rather than territorial;
- practical and action-oriented;
- serious about governance while still allowing warmth, creativity and glee.

The public site should feel credible to councils, government, funders and sponsors without losing the warmth and personality required to speak to regional communities.

## Confidentiality / publication guardrail
Do not assume a document is public merely because it exists in the project context or repository.

In particular, treat the following as non-public by default unless explicitly approved:
- detailed financial projections;
- proposed event locations and unannounced concepts;
- procurement thresholds and internal controls;
- risk registers and mitigation plans;
- internal governance templates/registers;
- unconfirmed board/member details;
- unconfirmed funding or sponsor targets;
- future expansion plans;
- internal contact details;
- draft legal/registration identifiers.

## Known inconsistencies and review flags
Do not resolve these autonomously:
- “Place” vs “Places” — website decision currently: **Place**.
- Board composition/office-bearer descriptions vary between source documents — verify before publication.
- Business Plan dating/year labels and FY labels should not be converted into public timelines without review.
- Constitution cover contains TBC registration identifiers — never publish placeholder identifiers as real identifiers.
- Strategic impact figures are future targets, not achieved statistics.
